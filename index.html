The short version

My Budget App keeps your money records on your own phone. There is no account to create, no server of ours to sign in to, and no copy of your data on any system we control. We cannot see what you record, and we could not hand it to anyone else even if we were asked.

Nothing you record leaves your phone unless you choose to send it: by exporting a backup file, by sending a problem report, by asking for a Smart insights summary (off until you turn it on), or — once it is available — by backing up to your own Google Drive.

What the app stores, and where

Everything you enter is written to a database file inside the app's private storage on your device:

  • the amounts, dates, categories, notes and payees you record;
  • the names of the people you add to your household;
  • your settings, including currency and whether you use Simple or Detailed mode;
  • any receipt images or files you attach to an entry;
  • your PIN, stored only as a salted PBKDF2-SHA256 hash — the PIN itself is never written down;
  • if you enable fingerprint or face unlock, a device token held in the Android Keystore.

Android's own app backup is switched off (allowBackup="false"), so the operating system does not copy this database to Google's servers on your behalf.

We receive none of it. The app has no analytics, no automatic crash reporting, no advertising, and no tracking of any kind. It does not build a profile of you and does not sell or share data. The only thing that can ever reach us is a problem report you decide to send, described below.

If something goes wrong

When the app hits an error it writes a short technical note — what failed and where in the code — to a file only this app can read. It is kept so that you can send it to us if you choose to: "Report a problem" in Settings — and on the lock screen, for the times the app will not open — shows you the exact text first and then lets you send it wherever you like. Nothing is sent on its own, amounts and names are removed before anything is written down, and you can clear the file at any time from the same screen.

When data leaves your device

1. Backup to your Google Drive — not yet available, and only if you turn it on

This option is not switched on in the current version of the app. When it is, and only if you connect Google Drive, the app packages your database and attachments, encrypts them on your device with AES-256-GCM using a passphrase you choose, and uploads only that encrypted file.

  • It goes to the app data folder of your own Google Drive, a private area only this app can read. It does not appear among your normal Drive files and is not shared with anyone.
  • Your passphrase never leaves your device and is never sent to Google or to us. If you forget it, the backup cannot be opened by anybody, including us.
  • To connect, you sign in with Google. The app asks for two things: permission to use that private app folder, and your email address so it can show you which account is connected. Your email is stored on your device only.
  • You can disconnect at any time in Backup & sync, and delete the backup files from your Google account whenever you wish.

Google processes this under its own privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy

2. Backup files you export

You can export an encrypted backup file from Backup & sync. It is encrypted on your device with a passphrase you choose, and it goes only where you send it — a folder, an email, a cloud drive of your choosing. We never receive it.

3. Smart insights (AI summaries) — only if you turn it on

Smart insights writes a short summary of your month and one tip, and answers questions you ask about your spending. It is off until you turn it on in Settings, and even then it sends something only when you tap "Write my AI summary" or send a question.

What is sent: the month, your totals for spending, money in and savings, your budget, and category names with amounts (a category name you typed yourself is sent as "Other"). Never sent: names, notes, dates of entries, account details, receipts, bank messages or statements, or anything about individual entries.

Where it goes depends on the service you choose:

  • Your own AI service. You pick a provider (for example Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, or a server you run yourself) and paste your own key. The summary figures go straight from your phone to that provider, under that provider's own privacy policy. Your key stays on your phone, in the Android Keystore, and is never included in a backup. We never see the figures or the key.
  • My Budget AI (with the Smart insights subscription). The summary figures go to our AI server, which passes them to the AI provider we use and returns the answer. With them, the app sends the Google Play purchase token of your subscription so our server can ask Google Play that it is active. Our server keeps no figures and no answers; it keeps only a count of summaries per subscription for the current month, stored under a one-way code made from the token, not the token itself. The AI provider we use processes the figures under its own terms to produce the answer.

Ask AI about my money (part of Smart insights) lets you ask questions in your own words. The AI cannot read your records. Instead it asks the app small questions, such as "total spent in September" or "top payees for Food", and the app answers each one on your phone. What is sent: your question as you typed it, and those answers - totals per category, per payee or per period. Payee names are replaced by "Payee 1", "Payee 2" and so on unless you turn on "Let the AI see payee names"; the real names are put back on your screen only. Every answer has a "What was shared" link showing exactly what was sent. Notes, dates of single entries, account details and family members' names are never sent. The AI cannot change anything in the app.

You can turn Smart insights off, or remove your key, at any time in Settings.

Nothing else

Apart from Google Drive backup (when available), Smart insights summaries you ask for, and any file or report you send yourself, the app makes no network requests. It asks for these Android permissions and no others:

PermissionWhy
INTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATEOnly for Google Drive backup and Smart insights, if you use them
USE_BIOMETRIC, USE_FINGERPRINTTo unlock the app with your fingerprint or face, if you choose
POST_NOTIFICATIONSTo show reminders you set on notes and to-dos
RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETEDTo keep those reminders after your phone restarts
com.android.vending.BILLINGTo let Google Play handle a purchase, if you choose to buy one

The app does not request location, contacts, camera, microphone, or access to your files beyond the ones you pick yourself when importing or attaching something. To photograph a receipt it hands off to your phone's camera app, which returns only the photo you take.

Paid features

Some features, such as extra household members, are unlocked by a purchase. Purchases are handled entirely by Google Play. You pay Google; we never see your card, your billing address, or your name. We keep no customer list and no record of who bought what: the app simply asks Google Play what your Google account owns and remembers the answer on your device, which is also why reinstalling or moving to a new phone restores what you bought. The one exception is the Smart insights subscription: to answer, our AI server asks Google Play whether your subscription is active, as described above.

Google's own handling of the payment is covered by Google's privacy policy, not this one.

Other people in your household

You can add family members by name, so you can record what they spend. They do not sign in and do not need a phone of their own. Anything you record about them is stored on your device under your control, on the same terms as your own data. Please add other people's details only if they are content for you to do so.

Children

The app is not directed at children, and a child does not need an account to be included: a parent can track a child's spending without the child ever being a user. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Keeping it safe

The database is encrypted on disk with AES-256 (SQLCipher). The key is generated on your device, kept in the Android Keystore, and never leaves it — so even someone who extracts the raw file from the phone cannot read your records. It sits in your phone's private app storage, which other apps cannot read. Your PIN is stored only as a salted hash, repeated wrong guesses are rate-limited, and backups are encrypted before they leave the device. No method of storage is perfectly secure, but because there is no central server holding everyone's records, there is no single place an attacker could go to reach other people's data.

How to delete your data — My Budget App

My Budget App (published by Snk) keeps your records on your phone, not with us. So most of your data you delete yourself, straight away, with no request needed.

To delete everything the app holds on your phone

  1. Optional: keep a copy first from Settings → Backup & sync → Export file.
  2. Uninstall My Budget App (or My Budget (Preview)) from your phone, or go to Android Settings → Apps → My Budget App → Storage → Clear storage.

This deletes all of it at once: entries, categories, budgets, goals, notes and to-dos, household members, attachments, settings, your PIN hash and the problem-report log. Nothing is kept, and there is no waiting period.

To delete single items instead, remove them inside the app; they are gone from your phone immediately.

To delete a problem report you sent us

  1. Email snktechdr@gmail.com with the subject "Delete my data – My Budget App".
  2. Send it from the same email address you sent the report from, so we can find it.

We delete the report and the email thread within 30 days and reply to confirm. We keep nothing afterwards. A problem report contains only technical error details; amounts and names are removed before it is written.

Backups you made yourself

Backup files you exported, and any Google Drive backup (once that option is available), are held in your own storage or your own Google account. Delete them there; we have no access to them.
